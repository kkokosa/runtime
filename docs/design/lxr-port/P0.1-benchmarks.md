# P0.1 — Reference benchmark results

Pause and throughput behaviour of the **reference** LXR implementation on both parity oracles, as a
sanity anchor for later .NET comparisons. **No .NET numbers appear in this step.**

The headline result is stated up front because it constrains everything downstream:

> **HEAD (`9625c174`) runs the full DaCapo 2006 set cleanly. PLDI (`4d4e516c`) does not.**
> PLDI completes single-iteration runs of 5 of 8 benchmarks but crashes with SIGSEGV under sustained
> execution. It is usable as a *short-observation* oracle only.

---

## 1. Method

| | |
|---|---|
| suite | **DaCapo 2006-10-MR2** — the common denominator; it is the suite the PLDI binding's own CI uses, and the head binding still carries `run-dacapo-2006.yml` |
| benchmarks | `antlr fop luindex lusearch pmd hsqldb eclipse xalan` — the 8 the reference CI enumerates |
| JVM flags | `-XX:+UseThirdPartyHeap -server -XX:MetaspaceSize=100M -Xms<H>M -Xmx<H>M` — copied verbatim from the reference's own `ci-test-normal.sh` |
| heaps | 500 MB and 2000 MB, **fixed** (see §2) |
| plan selection | PLDI: compile-time `GC_FEATURES=lxr,immix`. HEAD: runtime `MMTK_PLAN=LXR` |
| throughput | DaCapo's own `PASSED in N msec` for the final iteration |
| pause | mutator-observed stall meter (§3) |
| host | WSL2 Ubuntu 22.04 on Windows 11, 16 logical CPUs, 64 GB RAM |

**Passes run.**

| pass | build | `-n` | invocations | heaps | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | release | 5 | 3 | 500, 2000 | throughput + pause, standard DaCapo methodology |
| B | fastdebug | 1 | 1 | 2000 | **assertion** detection |
| C | release | 1 | 3 | 2000 | head-to-head at the iteration count PLDI can sustain |
| D | release | 1–5 | 1 | 2000 | iteration-scaling characterisation of the PLDI failure |

Chosen because pass A alone would have reported PLDI as almost entirely broken, which is true but not
the whole truth: passes C and D establish *how* it is broken, which is the part P0.3 needs.

### Caveats on the numbers

- The host is a WSL2 VM on a desktop, not a quiesced benchmarking machine. Treat these as **order of
  magnitude and shape**, not as publishable measurements. They exist to anchor "is a later .NET
  number in the right ballpark", not to reproduce the paper.
- DaCapo 2006 `msec` is one iteration's wall time, not a steady-state average.
- The two revisions run on **different OpenJDK builds** (`f817e9d0` vs `7caf8f7d`, four years apart)
  and different rustc. A PLDI-vs-HEAD throughput difference is therefore *not* attributable to the
  collector alone. This is unavoidable — neither binding builds against the other's JDK.

## 2. Why the heap is 500 MB and not a multiple of the minheap

Initially the heaps were derived as a multiple of the MarkCompact minheaps recorded in the PLDI
binding's own `common.sh` (antlr 5 MB, fop 13, luindex 6, lusearch 8, pmd 24, hsqldb 117, eclipse 23,
xalan 21). **At those sizes PLDI crashed on every benchmark**, which nearly produced the wrong
conclusion that the PLDI build was unusable.

The reference's own `ci-test-normal.sh` runs *every* DaCapo 2006 benchmark at
`-XX:MetaspaceSize=100M -Xms500M -Xmx500M`, with the comment:

> *"the command line options are necessary for now to ensure the benchmarks work"*

At 500 MB, PLDI passes. The minheap table describes what **MarkCompact** needs, not what LXR needs;
LXR at this revision requires far more headroom. That is itself a finding — recorded in §6.

## 3. How pauses were measured

There is **no symmetric GC log** across the two revisions. PLDI has `HARNESS_PRETTY_PRINT`,
`LOG_PER_GC_STATE` and `LOG_STAGES` behind a `log_gc` feature; HEAD has none of them. Neither
OpenJDK fork exposes a Java-visible `harness_begin`/`harness_end` entry point. Parsing GC logs would
therefore have compared two different things.

Instead pauses are measured **from the mutator's point of view**, jHiccup-style: a daemon thread
sleeps on a 1 ms cadence and records how far each wake-up overshoots its intended time. Any excess is
time the mutator was not permitted to run. For a collector with stop-the-world phases the tail of
that distribution is dominated by GC pauses.

This is deliberately the *pessimistic* and *portable* measure: it captures everything the application
actually feels — STW pauses, safepoint bias, and scheduler noise — and it will work identically
against a future .NET port, which is the point. It **over**-reports at the median (~0.12 ms of pure
timer jitter on this host, visible as the p50 floor in every row below), so **read the tail, not the
median**.

Sources: `scripts/mk-harness.sh` (`Hiccup.java`, `Bench.java`).

- **HEAD: met in full.** 8/8 benchmarks, no assertions, no crashes.

  rather than assert.

### Reading

1. **The common case is genuinely low-latency.** On six of eight benchmarks the p99 mutator stall is
   **well under 1 ms**, and on eclipse — the longest-running benchmark, 55 000+ samples per
   invocation — p99.9 at 2000 MB is **0.44–0.53 ms**. That is the LXR result the paper claims, and
   it reproduces here.
2. **hsqldb is the outlier on both revisions, and much worse on HEAD.** HEAD shows p99 ≈ 58–61 ms and
   a max of 130–360 ms; PLDI at `-n 1` shows p99.9 of 1.3 ms. The two are not measured on equal
   footing (PLDI cannot complete the `-n 5` run at 500 MB), so this is **not** a clean regression
   claim — but hsqldb is flagged as the benchmark to watch, and as the first place a .NET port should
   look for tail-latency trouble.
3. **lusearch and xalan have a mid-tail**, p99 of 4–11 ms, on HEAD. Both are heavily multi-threaded
   allocation-intensive benchmarks.
4. **Larger heaps flatten the tail** as expected: eclipse p99.9 drops from ~5.5 ms at 500 MB to
   ~0.5 ms at 2000 MB.
5. **PLDI's tails are tighter than HEAD's on every benchmark it completes** — but only over `-n 1`
   runs, which is a fraction of the GC activity. Do not read this as PLDI having better pause
   behaviour.

## 7. The PLDI failure, characterised

### 7.1 Signature

Two faulting sites, alternating between runs of the *same* configuration:

```
C  [libmmtk_openjdk.so+0x139c20]
   <mmtk::plan::barriers::FieldLoggingBarrier<E> as mmtk::plan::barriers::Barrier>::write_barrier

C  [libmmtk_openjdk.so+0x189434]
   <mmtk::util::cm::ImmixConcurrentTraceObjects<VM> as mmtk::scheduler::work::GCWork<VM>>::do_work
```

The mutator write barrier and the concurrent marking trace — i.e. **the two ends of the SATB
handshake**. That the faulting site is not deterministic for a fixed configuration is itself
evidence: this is a race, not a deterministic bug.

### 7.2 Iteration scaling — the decisive experiment

Same benchmark, same heap (2000 MB), only `-n` varies:

| `-n` | PLDI antlr | HEAD antlr |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | OK | OK |
| 2 | OK (1 warm-up completed) | — |
| 3 | OK (2 warm-ups completed) | OK |
| 4 | OK (3 warm-ups completed) | — |
| 5 | **CRASH** — after completing 4 warm-ups | OK |

PLDI survives four full iterations of antlr and dies in the fifth. HEAD is unaffected at every point.

**This is a survival-time signature, not a workload signature.** It is consistent with slowly
accumulating state corruption — the longer the collector runs, the more likely the fault — and it is
inconsistent with "antlr does something LXR cannot handle", since antlr does the same thing five
times.

### 7.3 What was ruled out, by experiment not by reasoning

| hypothesis | test | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Not actually running LXR | `RUST_LOG=info` startup banner | **ruled out.** `ix_ref_count`, `ix_concurrent_marking`, `lxr_lazy_decrements`, `lxr_nursery_evacuation`, `lxr_mature_evacuation` all `true`; `barrier: "FieldLoggingBarrier"` |
| Wrong mmtk-core revision | rebuilt against the binding's own pin `df8d30a3` | **ruled out** — identical crash |
| Compressed oops | read the binding | **ruled out** — it sets `UseCompressedOops=false` itself and asserts on it |
| Heap too small | 15 / 50 / 100 / 200 / 500 / 1000 / 2000 MB | **partly the cause** — 500 MB+ is necessary but not sufficient |
| A hang | `RUST_LOG=info` + timing | **ruled out** — a crash; the 155 s wall time was WSL core-dump capture, removed by resetting `core_pattern` |
| Assertion / invariant violation | fastdebug builds | **ruled out** — no assertion fired |

### 7.4 Two of the three failures are already known-broken in the reference

PLDI's own `ci-test-normal.sh` **comments out** several benchmarks with reasons:

```
#... lusearch - validation failed
#... xalan    - mmtk-core gets stuck in slowdebug build
#... bloat    - does not work for stock build
#... jython   - does not work for stock build
```

So **lusearch and xalan are on the reference's own broken list at this revision, for stock plans**
— they are not LXR regressions. **eclipse is not**: it is in the actively-tested CI list for
SemiSpace, Immix and GenImmix, so its failure under LXR is a genuine LXR-specific defect.

Reduced honestly, the LXR-specific single-iteration failure at PLDI is **one benchmark: eclipse**.
The multi-iteration instability, however, affects nearly everything.

### 7.5 Neither revision's LXR path is covered by the reference's own CI

- PLDI `ci-test-normal.sh` tests SemiSpace, Immix, GenImmix. Not LXR.
- HEAD `.github/configs/normal-heap.yml` lists nine plans. None is LXR.

Nobody upstream is running these tests. The instability found here was never going to be caught by
the reference's CI, and "the reference works" is not something CI attests to for LXR on either
revision.

## 8. Consequences for P0.3

1. **HEAD is the only oracle that can be *observed* across the full benchmark set.** PLDI can be
   observed for short runs of 5 benchmarks and read for everything else.
2. Every mechanism recommendation in `P0.1-mechanism-diff.md` that selects **PLDI** —
   notably §5 (SATB concurrent marking semantics) and §8 (evacuation policy) — rests substantially on
   **code reading**, and must carry that caveat into the ledger.
3. **Do not treat PLDI's numbers as the paper's numbers.** The paper's evaluation used a quiesced
   machine, `running-ng`, many invocations, and heap sizes expressed as multiples of a measured
   minheap. This step reproduces the *shape* — sub-millisecond p99 on most benchmarks — not the
   magnitudes.
4. **A .NET port should target HEAD's behaviour as the pass/fail bar** and use PLDI only where a
   mechanism's *definition* is clearer there.
5. **hsqldb tail latency** is the specific behaviour to watch: it is the one place the reference
   itself shows 100 ms+ mutator stalls.
